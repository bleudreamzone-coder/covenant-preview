The quiet leak

Money leaves quietly.
Wolf watches, so you don’t have to.

Most people don’t lose money in one dramatic moment. It drips — a duplicate charge nobody notices, a renewal nobody set, a fee line nobody reads, an obligation due the same week as the plan. The drip is the whole problem, and nobody has time to sit and stare at it. That is the job.

He watches the drip

Statements, subscriptions, obligations, markets — read line by line, every week, without getting tired or bored. When a charge appears twice, he says so, shows you both lines, and waits for your word. One duplicate in this demo is $24 a month — counted as potential savings until a later statement proves it gone, never before.

He watches the markets

Prices move while you are living your life. Wolf keeps the observatory — indices, volume, the notes that matter — and brings you only what changes a decision. No flashing tickers, no panic, no casino. A calm page that says, in words, what moved and what it means for you.

Control is the luxury

Every plan shows its evidence and its expiry. Every limit is one you set, and no lesson, model, or market can raise it. Pause is one tap, everywhere, forever. The most expensive thing in finance is a decision you did not understand — here you understand every one, or it does not run.

Lineage

One species. Your own Wolf.

“Your records stay private. Verified lessons can improve future versions.”

Your Wolf keeps a private memory that belongs to you. When the species learns something — a trap pattern, a failed strategy, a better check — the lesson is stripped of account details, reviewed, replayed against counterexamples, and independently verified before any Wolf may adopt it. You see the gate. You choose adoption where your settings require it.

🔒 Private by default

Customer-local memory

Your goals, your mandate, your history — stored under your scope alone. No other customer’s Wolf can read it. Exports and deletion live in your Settings, not in a support ticket.

⚖ Reviewed before shared

The lesson review gate

A candidate lesson carries its hypothesis, its evidence, its failures and its counterexamples. It passes replay and an independent verifier — or it is rejected, with the reason kept on record.

⤴ Versioned, reversible

Version updates

An adopted lesson arrives as a named version — for example wolf-core 1.4.0 — with a changelog and a rollback. Updates change knowledge. They never change your limits.

What crosses between customers is the verified lesson — never raw account data. The full gate, with one accepted and one rejected candidate, is inspectable in the workspace under .

The Silent Thief

Money that sleeps is money leaking.

Nobody steals it in the night. It leaves in daylight, a fraction at a time, out of the accounts nobody is watching — because money that is not working is still charged rent. The rent is called inflation, and around here it has a name: the silent thief. No statement ever prints his line. He takes his cut from what your money can buy, and the balance — the number everyone watches — keeps smiling.

Checking

Everyday money, sleeping at nearly nothing while prices climb past it.

Savings

The “safe” account — safe from everything except the thief, who is already inside.

CDs

Locked up for a year or more — and the lock does not stop the leak.

IRAs

Retirement accounts that stopped working — cash sitting inside a working account’s clothes.

⌕

Wolf’s job is the hunt. He reads where your money sleeps — checking, savings, CDs, IRAs that stopped working — prices the leak account by account, and shows you the choice: what could move, where it could go, and what it would cost. Then he waits for your word. He never moves money, trades money, or makes money for you, or executes anything without your permission.

How every claim is built

Observed, then said
A statement names its source, its observation time and its retrieval time — separately.
Price ≠ forecast

Straight answers

Questions, answered like Wolf answers

Does Wolf hold my money?+

By design, no. In the proposed architecture your funds remain with your own provider — your bank or broker holds your money; Wolf works through permissions you grant, and in this preview he holds nothing at all: no accounts are connected and no real orders exist. Covenant makes no custody claim beyond what the actual architecture supports.

Can he act without asking each time?+

Only inside a workflow you specifically activated, within its permitted limits — and only when that capability is legally and technically available. Outside an activated mandate, Wolf proposes; you decide. In this concept preview, the only “acting” available is paper simulation.

Does learning change my limits?+

No. A model or strategy update cannot increase financial permission — not your limits, not your accounts, not your mode. Learning changes what Wolf knows. Only you change what Wolf may do, and every such change is versioned and shown back to you.

Is profit guaranteed?+

No. Research and automation can lose money. Any Wolf, plan or page that promises a return — daily percentages included — is describing something other than honest finance. Covenant shows drawdowns, losing intervals and failed candidates alongside the wins, or it does not show results at all.

What data is shared?+

Your records stay in your scope. The only thing that can leave is a verified lesson — an anonymized pattern with its evidence and counterexamples — and only under your consent settings, which are separate from core product access and off unless you turn them on. This mockup uses synthetic records only; no real customer data exists in it.

Can I leave?+

Yes. Settings & Exit gives you export of your records, disconnect of provider scopes, and session revocation. Disconnect stops future access immediately. Any outstanding orders at a provider are handled explicitly — they are listed, not silently cancelled or silently left running, and their state is shown until each one resolves.

What is available today?+

Today, this: a concept preview with simulated data. Research and paper planning are demonstrated with fixtures; live execution, withdrawals and real connections are unavailable. Covenant claims operational access only where a screen can show its evidence — and in this preview, no screen can, because none exists yet.

Evidence drawer · Case SIM-RCPT-2026-1042

The $24 duplicate — source & methodology

This drawer is the receipt behind the claim. In a live Covenant, every statement Wolf makes opens a drawer like this one.

Source records (fixtures)

  • Transaction line T-8812 — Oct 3, 2026 · “Northbeam Storage” · direct debit · $24.00
  • Transaction line T-8840 — Oct 17, 2026 · “Northbeam Storage via AppStore” · $24.00
  • Service account check — one active storage plan found, not two (sample provider response)

Method

  • Merchant names normalized across rails before matching (direct vs app-store descriptors differ).
  • Match requires same merchant, same amount, same billing cadence, and a single active service.
  • Confidence: high on duplication; deliberately not stated on whether the service itself is wanted — that is the customer’s call.

Times, kept separate

  • Charge times: Oct 3 and Oct 17, 2026 (provider records).
  • Retrieval time: Oct 21, 2026, 09:14 CT (when Wolf read them in this fixture).
  • Confirmation: Oct 21, 2026, by the customer, in the demo.

What would change the conclusion

  • A second genuinely distinct storage plan on the account.
  • Different service tiers or storage volumes behind the two charges.

Accounting treatment

Counted as potential savings of $24/month. It becomes realized savings only when a later statement verifies the charge is absent. It is never annualized into a “recovered” headline before then.
